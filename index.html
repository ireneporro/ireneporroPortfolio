How I work

I do not start with screens.

I start by finding the decision hidden inside the request. That means understanding the business context, the people involved, what can go wrong and what the team needs to keep moving.

  1. 01

    Frame the problem

    Clarify the real need, the constraints and whose decision the product has to support.

  2. 02

    Model the system

    Map roles, flows, states and dependencies before they turn into screen-level inconsistencies.

  3. 03

    Make decisions tangible

    Use flows, interface design and prototypes to test the logic and give the team something concrete to challenge.

  4. 04

    Stay close to delivery

    Work through edge cases and implementation questions so the product keeps its intent after handoff.

Designing with purpose

About

I like making sense of messy systems.

I am Irene Porro, a Senior Product Designer with 5+ years of experience across B2B SaaS, platform products, dashboards, CRM systems and data-heavy interfaces.

I am most useful when requirements arrive as a list of features, different people hold different parts of the context and the product still needs to feel simple. I find the structure, make the trade-offs visible and turn them into something the team can discuss and build.

I care about visual craft, but not as decoration. Hierarchy, interaction and consistency are part of how a product communicates. The small details matter because they are where users understand what happened and what to do next.

How I judge the work

A polished screen is not enough.

I look at whether the product explains itself, survives edge cases and gives the people building it a clear, reusable logic.

Product quality

I look for what breaks when the happy path ends.

  • The next action is clear without extra explanation.
  • Empty, loading, permission and error states are intentional.
  • The same product rule behaves consistently across flows.
  • Engineering can understand the decision, not only copy the screen.
Consistency

Patterns should remove decisions.

A shared pattern is useful when it reduces ambiguity for users and the team, not simply because it looks the same.

Delivery

Handoff is part of the design.

Implementation questions often reveal missing logic, so I stay involved while the experience becomes a real product.

The goal

Make the next decision easier.

For the user and for the team building the product.
